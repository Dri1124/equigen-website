01 / Our Purpose

Black Women's Health Is Our Motivation

Health knowledge can change the course of a life—and sometimes an entire family's future.

Yet many communities face barriers to early screening, genetic counseling, and access to advances in preventive medicine. For Black women, these challenges intersect with longstanding disparities in cardiovascular disease and breast cancer outcomes.

EquiGen exists to help bridge the gap between scientific discovery and community understanding. We aim to help individuals and families recognize inherited risks, ask informed questions, and participate confidently in decisions about their health.

02 / Why It Matters

The Numbers Tell Part of the Story

Health outcomes reflect access, lived experiences, environmental conditions, healthcare systems, and biology—not genetics alone.

#1

Heart disease

Heart disease is the leading cause of death among Black women in the United States. Knowing cholesterol levels and family history can help start conversations about inherited cardiovascular risk.

Source: CDC, Women and Heart Disease; see CDC heart disease information.

Higher breast cancer mortality

Black women experience substantially higher breast cancer death rates than White women despite somewhat lower overall incidence. Timely screening, quality care, and appropriate risk assessment matter.

03 / Our Commitment

Science Should Benefit the Communities That Helped Build It

Scientific progress has been shaped by generations of individuals and communities whose contributions were not always acknowledged, consensual, or fairly recognized.

From Henrietta Lacks, whose cells transformed biomedical research, to countless individuals whose biological samples and participation advanced medical knowledge, these contributions are part of the foundation of modern medicine.

We believe the communities that helped make scientific progress possible should not be the last to benefit from it. EquiGen is committed to honoring history while making genetic knowledge more accessible for future generations.

06 / Understanding the Bigger Picture

Genetics Is One Piece of a Much Larger Health Story

While EquiGen's educational resources highlight hereditary breast cancer and familial hypercholesterolemia (FH), it's important to understand that not all breast cancers are hereditary, and FH is not the only cause of high cholesterol or cardiovascular disease.

Breast cancer can develop without an inherited cancer-predisposing variant. Likewise, heart disease can result from a combination of genetic, environmental, behavioral, and social factors.

Our focus on these inherited conditions is intended to increase awareness of genetic risk, encourage informed conversations with healthcare professionals, and promote prevention and early detection—not to suggest that genetics alone determines health outcomes.

Your genes matter, but they are not your entire health story.
