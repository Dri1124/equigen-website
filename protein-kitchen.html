From instructions to action

What happens after a gene is read?

For protein-coding genes, a cell uses DNA information to make an RNA message, then uses that message to build a chain of amino acids. The chain folds into a protein with a particular shape and function.

Why a protein's shape matters

Imagine a kitchen tool designed to fit one task. If its shape changes, it may work normally, less effectively, differently, or not at all. Proteins also depend on their three-dimensional structure and interactions with other molecules.

Functional protein ✓

Its structure supports the job it needs to perform.

Altered protein ?

Some changes affect function; many have little or no meaningful effect. The impact depends on the specific change.

Connection to EquiGen's other modules: LDL receptors help liver cells remove LDL particles from blood. BRCA1, BRCA2 and PALB2 proteins help cells repair DNA damage. Different proteins, different jobs—and different health consequences when those jobs are disrupted.
